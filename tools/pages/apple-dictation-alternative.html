Troubleshooting

Apple Dictation not working? Fixes first, options after

macOS has dictation built in, it is free, and when it misbehaves the problem is usually fixable in two minutes. Start here. If none of it helps, the last section is honest about when the built-in feature is simply not the right tool.

The five fixes worth trying, in order

1. Check that the right language is selected — and downloaded

Open System Settings → Keyboard → Dictation. The language list there is what actually decides recognition quality. If you speak French but the list says English (US), everything you say is being forced through the wrong model. Adding a language may trigger a download; on Apple Silicon that download is what enables on-device dictation, which is both more private and more reliable than the network path.

2. Check which microphone macOS is really using

System Settings → Sound → Input. A Bluetooth headset that connected for a call, a webcam mic across the room, or a monitor's built-in microphone will all quietly win over the good one. Speak and watch the input level move. If it barely moves, dictation is not going to work no matter which app you use.

3. Turn dictation off and on again

Genuinely. Toggling the Dictation switch in System Settings forces macOS to re-initialise the service and re-check its language assets. This clears a surprising share of "it stopped listening after one sentence" cases.

4. Sort out the punctuation setting

Recent macOS versions insert punctuation automatically for supported languages, and there is a toggle for it in the Dictation settings. If you are getting a single unbroken sentence, that toggle is off — or your language is not one of the supported ones, in which case you have to say "comma" and "period" out loud. If instead you are getting commas you never asked for, that same toggle being on is the cause.

5. Restart, then check Screen Time and MDM

A restart clears a stuck dictation daemon. If dictation is greyed out entirely, look at Screen Time content restrictions, and — on a work Mac — at whether your employer's device management has disabled it. That last one you cannot fix yourself.

What Apple Dictation will not do, however you configure it

These are design limits, not bugs, and no amount of settings-fiddling changes them:

  • It does not learn your vocabulary. A colleague's name, a product name, an industry term — it will get them wrong today and wrong again next month.
  • It will not transcribe a file. There is no way to hand it an existing recording of a meeting or an interview.
  • It keeps no history. If the text lands in the wrong window or an app rejects it, it is gone.
  • You cannot choose the engine. When accuracy matters more than speed, there is no lever to pull.
  • No speaker separation. For anything with more than one voice, it is the wrong tool entirely.

Questions

Why does Apple Dictation stop after a sentence or two?

Most often the wrong microphone is selected, or the language pack has not finished downloading so it is falling back to the network path. Check Sound → Input, then re-add your language in Keyboard → Dictation and toggle dictation off and on.

Why is there no punctuation?

Automatic punctuation is a setting, and it only covers some languages. If yours is not supported you have to say the punctuation out loud. Aria restores capitals and full stops locally without being asked.

Does dictation work without an internet connection?

Apple's does on Apple Silicon once the language is downloaded. Aria's local engine does too, on both Apple Silicon and Intel.

Can I improve accuracy on names and jargon?

Not meaningfully with the built-in feature. Aria adds the words you correct to a personal dictionary, so the same fix does not have to be made twice.
