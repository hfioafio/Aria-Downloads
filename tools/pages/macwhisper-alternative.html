Compare both workflows

Ask what you do most often. For importing recordings and editing transcripts, compare MacWhisper’s file tools. For drafting a long email at the cursor, compare both apps’ dictation shortcuts and modes.

Aria covers both, with the emphasis reversed: dictation first, files second. For a lot of people the file work is occasional and the dictation is daily.

What Aria adds for daily dictation

  • Faithful by default. Capitals and full stops are restored locally; nothing is summarised, translated or restyled unless you pick that mode.
  • Language preserved. Recognition targets the spoken language; choose an explicit language if detection is wrong.
  • A dictionary that learns. Aria retains corrected terms to guide later recognition. Review the result, especially names.
  • Recoverable text. If the target app refuses the insertion, the transcript is still in your history rather than gone.
  • A local mode you choose. After the model download, audio and transcription are processed on your Mac. Quota or licence checks may still need a connection.

Compare the file workflow. If you mostly work through batches of recordings, look at MacWhisper’s organisation and editing tools. Aria also exports TXT, SRT and VTT; try a representative file before choosing.

Questions

Can Aria replace MacWhisper for file transcription?

For occasional files, yes — it handles the common audio and video formats and labels speakers. For heavy, daily file work, MacWhisper is the more specialised tool.

Does Aria run Whisper too?

Yes, Whisper is available among the local models alongside Parakeet. Automatic mode uses the online service supplied by Aria during the beta, without a key and with limits.

Can I buy Aria Pro?

Purchases are closed during the current beta. You can try the free allowance of 2,000 words a week.
