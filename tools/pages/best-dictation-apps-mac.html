Start with the free one that is already installed

Apple Dictation works offline on Apple Silicon once its language pack is downloaded, and for short bursts it is fine. Try it before paying anyone. People leave it for three reasons, in order: punctuation, proper nouns it will never learn, and the fact that it cannot touch an existing recording. If none of those bite you, stop here — you are done.

The question that actually decides it

Not "which is best". These apps split cleanly along two lines.

Do you dictate, or do you transcribe? Dictating means talking and having text appear where you are working — Aria, Wispr Flow, Superwhisper and VoiceInk are built for that. Transcribing means feeding in a recording — MacWhisper is the specialist. Aria does both, with the emphasis on dictation.

Does your voice leave the machine? Aria, MacWhisper, VoiceInk and Superwhisper can all run the model locally. Wispr Flow cannot: it is cloud-only by architecture, with no offline mode. If you dictate anything confidential, that single fact decides the shortlist.

Three years of dictation, in money

  • Apple Dictation: €0
  • Aria: €5, once, three Macs
  • VoiceInk: $25 to $49, once
  • MacWhisper Pro: €64, once
  • Superwhisper Pro monthly: about $306
  • Wispr Flow Pro yearly: about $432

A subscription is not unreasonable when the vendor pays a cloud model per minute — that is genuinely their cost. It is worth knowing which side of that line you are buying on.

Where Aria loses

  • macOS only. No Windows, no iPhone. If you switch machines during the day, Superwhisper or Wispr Flow fit better.
  • Not the file specialist. For hours of audio, batches, and subtitle work, MacWhisper is the more focused tool.
  • No custom AI prompts. Aria ships a deliberately small set of transformations. Superwhisper lets you write your own.
  • No enterprise paperwork. No SSO, no SOC 2, no HIPAA. That is Wispr Flow's territory.
  • Not notarised by Apple yet. The first launch needs System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway. It is one step, once, and it is a real annoyance.
  • Small and new. One developer, no track record, no reviews yet. That is a legitimate reason to wait.

Where Aria wins

If you are on a Mac, dictate daily, refuse a subscription and would rather your voice not travel: €5 once, a local engine, text typed into any app, a dictionary that learns your names, file transcription with speaker labels, and a searchable local history. That combination does not currently exist anywhere else at that price.

Questions

What is the best free dictation app for Mac?

Apple Dictation if you dictate rarely — it is built in and offline. For daily use with correct punctuation, a personal dictionary and file transcription, Aria is free up to 2,000 words a week, and MacWhisper has a free version aimed at files.

Which Mac dictation apps work offline?

Apple Dictation on Apple Silicon, plus Aria, MacWhisper, VoiceInk and Superwhisper, which all run models locally. Wispr Flow does not — it is cloud-only.

Which one has no subscription?

Aria (€5 once), MacWhisper (€64 once) and VoiceInk ($25–$49 once). Superwhisper and Wispr Flow are monthly, though Superwhisper also sells a lifetime licence.

Do any of them type into any app?

Aria, Wispr Flow, Superwhisper and VoiceInk insert text at the cursor. MacWhisper is built around files instead.
